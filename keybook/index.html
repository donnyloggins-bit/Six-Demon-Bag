The scale

A minor

What does this mean?

The scale

The set of notes a song in this key mostly uses. The gold box is the root, the note that feels like home.

The numbers under each note

Scale degrees: where each note sits compared with the major scale. “♭3” means one half step lower than the major scale’s 3rd. That one note is what makes minor sound minor.

W and H

Whole step = 2 frets, or skip one piano key. Half step = 1 fret, or the very next key. Every scale is a pattern of these two sizes.

Key signature

Which notes stay sharp or flat all through the key. You don’t need to read music for this to help: it tells you which in-between notes are “in”.

Related keys

Relative keys use the same notes and chords with a different home, like C major and A minor. Parallel keys share the home note but use different notes, like A major and A minor.

Tip

Press Play scale and sing along with the numbers “1 2 3 4 5 6 7 1”. It trains your ear to recognize scale degrees in real songs.

What does this mean?

Reading the neck

It’s drawn like tab: the top line is the thin high e string, the bottom line is the thick low E. Numbers along the bottom are frets, with 0 for open strings. Gold dots are the root.

Positions

The scale split into hand-sized shapes you can play without sliding. Neighboring positions share notes, so learn one, then the one next to it, and you’ll connect the whole neck.

Note names or scale degrees

Switch to scale degrees to see where the 1, 3 and 5 sit. Those are the chord tones, the safest notes to land on at the end of a lick.

How to practice it

Pick one position. Play it slowly up and down, starting and ending on a root, with a metronome. Then improvise using only that box over a chord. Move on when it feels automatic.

The scale on the keys

Gold numbers are fingerings for one octave, root to root. 1 is the thumb, 5 the pinky. Tap any key to hear it.

Fingering
What does this mean?

Finger numbers

1 thumb, 2 index, 3 middle, 4 ring, 5 pinky. Both hands use the same numbers.

Why the thumb tucks under

A scale has eight notes and you have five fingers. Going up, the right thumb passes under the hand so you can keep going without a jump. Coming down, finger 3 or 4 crosses back over the thumb.

The fingering table

The rows give the full fingering for one octave in each hand. The thumb almost never lands on a black key, so scales with more black keys start on other fingers.

How to practice it

Hands separately, slowly, with the same fingering every time. Your hand memorizes the shape; speed comes later on its own.

What does this mean?

Reading a chord diagram

Vertical lines are strings, low E on the left. Horizontal lines are frets; the thick bar on top is the nut. Dots show where fingers go and the gold dot is the root. O = play open, X = don’t play. “5fr” means the diagram starts at fret 5. A long bar is a barre: one finger flat across several strings.

Roman numerals

The chord’s position in the key. Uppercase (IV) is major, lowercase (ii) is minor, ° is diminished. Musicians say “the one, the four, the five”. A ♭ in front means it’s built on a lowered note, like ♭VII in rock.

Triads and 7th chords

Triads are the basic three-note chords. 7th chords add one more note for a richer sound: jazzy (maj7, m7) or bluesy (7). Either set fits the key.

Harmony map

What works together

Every chord in a key has a job: it feels like home, it moves away, or it builds tension that wants to resolve. Good progressions move between those jobs.

What does this mean?

Home, Away, Tension

Home chords feel settled. Away chords travel. Tension chords feel unfinished and want to go home. V to I is the strongest move in music.

Strong, common, surprise

These ratings come from centuries of songwriting habits. Rock and pop break them all the time. A “surprise” isn’t wrong; it just grabs attention. Trust your ear over the label.

Color chords

A secondary dominant is a major or 7th chord from outside the key that points at a chord inside it, like a mini V-to-I. A borrowed chord comes from the parallel major or minor key, like the ♭VI and ♭VII chords all over rock.

Songwriting tip

Start and end on home, use away chords to travel, and put a tension chord right before the moment you want to feel like an arrival, such as the first beat of a chorus.
